Alley 21 is pursuing several related but distinct objectives. Some are operational businesses, some are working software products, and others are research proposals or potential academic partnerships. They should not all be described as completed accomplishments.

Start with a worthwhile problem, preserve authoritative information, make uncertainty visible, define when human review is required, and test small implementations before scaling.

The Woven Map research corpus

A substantial body of research specifications and conceptual work on symbolic empiricism, falsifiability, retrospective fitting, evidence provenance, and AI-generated interpretations, including:

  • Master Canonical Reference
  • Master Operating Contract v6.2
  • Discrimination Gate
  • Behavioral Symbolic Reconstruction

These constitute a developing research program, not a collection of confirmed peer-reviewed publications. The newer Discrimination Gate is particularly relevant to publication: it proposes comparing a context-only AI response with responses using authentic and alternative symbolic data — an experimental design that could examine whether added symbolic material contributes genuinely distinctive information or merely changes the persuasiveness of the prose. A second list, checked — affiliation-focused venues

A separate sweep (via ChatGPT, adjudicated against the publishers’ own policies on November 12) asked a narrower question: which journals accommodate independent researchers? Its central distinction holds — a journal asking for an affiliation is not necessarily requiring university employment.

  • AI for Society (MDPI) — explicitly accepts concept papers; scope covers AI philosophy, human subjectivity, knowledge production, and AI ethics — a genuine fit. MDPI’s “Independent Researcher” affiliation wording is verified across MDPI journal instructions, but was verified on the AI journal’s page, not separately on AI for Society’s — its editorial office can confirm at aisoc@mdpi.com. The cost the sweep omitted: fully open access with an APC of 1,000 CHF charged on acceptance — the main practical fact about this venue for an unfunded independent author.
  • Technophany — author biography required, supplied separately from the anonymized manuscript; no institutional-affiliation prerequisite stated. Guidelines also give: 5,000–8,000 words including notes and bibliography, ~150-word abstract, Chicago notes-and-bibliography style. Contact: journal@philosophyandtechnology.network.
  • Constructivist Foundations — requires affiliation in manuscript metadata; whether “Independent Researcher” satisfies it needs confirmation (submission@constructivist.info). A reported ban on generative AI writing any part of a manuscript is unverified — the journal’s own instruction pages did not surface; the editorial office’s reply would settle it, and it matters given how the corpus was developed.
  • Journal of Constructivist Psychology — invites scholars and practitioners across disciplines; no affiliation prerequisite found. The Humanistic Psychologist, Journal of Humanistic Psychology, and Semiotica state title-page affiliation requirements with no explicit independent-author exception located — on their face these are metadata requirements, not eligibility bars.

Business structure & money

Alley 21 Enterprises LLC vs. Extra Mile Tech

Earlier business planning established Extra Mile Tech as the technical-services operation working with Alley 21 — Daniel’s brand (dhcross.com), framed as holder of the code, prompts, and repository, licensing work to Alley 21. The October records clarify an important legal distinction:

  • Alley 21 Enterprises LLC is the Florida entity — Doc. #L26000185075, active, filed March 31, 2026. Stephanie Breeden is registered agent and authorized member; Daniel is an authorized person, not a member.
  • Extra Mile Tech has no Sunbiz record — it is a trade name / service identity, not a registered entity. That means Daniel personally is the counterparty to any licensing or revenue agreement unless he forms an entity first.
  • The business plans distinguish technical consulting, operational support, ownership of developed software, licensing, and commercial exploitation — and specifically sought to keep Thrift Companion ownership and its business-use rights separate from payment for ordinary technical services. (Historical planning point: Thrift Companion is no longer in active development as of November 2026.)

This matters to research publication as well: software ownership, research authorship, academic affiliations, and commercial licensing are different rights and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Assessment of the direction

Alley 21 currently contains three potentially sustainable lines of business: professional environmental/regulatory services, applied AI products and consulting, and education/research collaboration. There is a coherent connection between them — the same constraints developed for Raven (authoritative inputs, source traceability, calibrated uncertainty, restricted automation, human oversight) apply to regulatory document analysis, business decision tools, and responsible AI education.

However, the research program benefits from a clearer distinction between commercial product demonstrations, methodological proposals, completed experiments, and externally reviewed publications — which is how this page labels things throughout. The strategic opportunity is to establish Alley 21 not merely as a company that uses AI, but as a company specializing in accountable AI-assisted work — where evidence, model behavior, and human authority remain distinguishable. That is supported by the documented portfolio; whether it becomes Alley 21’s primary market position remains a business decision rather than a demonstrated outcome.
